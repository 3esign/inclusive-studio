Reference · design, rules, limits, law

By age.

Everything in this course that changes when the user’s age changes, on one page: ten bands of development, the limits that attach to each, and the law behind them. Three classes of statement are kept apart — an obligation, a technical limit with its test, and an observation about development — because quoting one as another is the most common mistake in a studio and the easiest to catch.

  1. Age is a proxy; behaviour is the measurement. The hazard band follows what the child does with the object, and the age on the label is the second line.
  2. Every number has an edition and a date. EN 71-1 has a 2026 edition; Regulation (EU) 2025/2509 repeals the toy directive and applies from 1 August 2030.
  3. Law, standard and development data never share a column.

Three limits that carry the first two weeks: a loose part for a child under 36 months may not fit entirely inside the small-parts cylinder of ⌀31,7 mm — EN 71-1, clause 8.2 ↗; a tangling cord on a toy for a child under 18 months is limited to 220 mm; and on a public playground in Serbia no equipment may have a free height of fall over 3 m, with the age band it is meant for written on a board of at least 1,5 × 1,5 m at the entrance — Pravilnik o bezbednosti dečjih igrališta, Sl. glasnik RS 41/2019, čl. 10 and 11 ↗.
